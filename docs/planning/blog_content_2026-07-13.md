## Article 1: How to Read Your Health Insurance Policy in 5 Minutes

Most people never read their health insurance policy until they need to file a claim — and by then it's too late to discover what's not covered. Here's a 5-minute guide to understanding your policy.

### The 5 Key Sections

1. **Policy Schedule** — This is page 1. It has your policy number, sum insured, premium, and policy period. If nothing else, save a screenshot of this page.

2. **Coverage/Benefits** — What the insurer will pay for. Look for: hospitalization, daycare procedures, pre/post-hospitalization expenses, ambulance, room rent.

3. **Exclusions** — What the insurer will NOT pay for. This is the most important section. Common exclusions: pre-existing conditions (usually after a waiting period), cosmetic treatments, self-inflicted injuries, war-related injuries.

4. **Waiting Periods** — Time you must wait before certain coverages activate. Typically: 30 days initial waiting period, 1-2 years for specific treatments, 2-4 years for pre-existing conditions.

5. **Claims Process** — How to file a claim. Look for: cashless vs reimbursement, network hospital list, helpline number, email, required documents.

## Article 2: What Is a Deductible? (And Why It Matters)

A deductible is the amount you pay out of pocket before your insurance kicks in. If your deductible is ₹5,000 and your hospital bill is ₹50,000, you pay ₹5,000 and the insurer pays ₹45,000.

### Types of Deductibles

- **Voluntary deductible** — You choose a higher deductible for a lower premium. Good if you rarely need medical care.
- **Compulsory deductible** — Fixed by the insurer, not optional.
- **Per-claim deductible** — Applied each time you file a claim.
- **Annual deductible** — Applied once per policy year.

### Why It Matters

A higher deductible means:
- Lower premium (you pay less per year)
- Higher out-of-pocket cost when you need care
- You self-insure for small expenses

A lower deductible means:
- Higher premium (you pay more per year)
- Lower out-of-pocket cost when you need care
- The insurer covers more of your expenses

## Article 3: 5 Coverage Gaps You Probably Have in Your Insurance

Most people think they're fully covered. They're usually wrong. Here are 5 common coverage gaps:

### 1. No Critical Illness Rider
Your health insurance covers hospitalization, but what about cancer, heart attack, or stroke? A critical illness rider pays a lump sum on diagnosis — your base policy doesn't.

### 2. No Roadside Assistance in Auto Insurance
You have auto insurance, but what happens when your car breaks down on the highway? Roadside assistance is often an add-on, not included by default.

### 3. No Personal Accident Cover
Your auto insurance covers damage to your car and third-party liability, but what about injury to you? Personal accident cover is separate.

### 4. Maternity Sub-Limit
Your health insurance may cover maternity, but with a sub-limit (e.g., ₹40,000). Actual maternity costs can be ₹50,000-₹1,00,000.

### 5. No Mental Health Coverage
Most Indian health insurance policies don't explicitly cover mental health treatment, despite the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 mandating parity.

## Article 4: How to File an Insurance Claim: A Step-by-Step Guide

Filing an insurance claim doesn't have to be stressful. Here's exactly what to do:

### Health Insurance Claim

**Cashless (at network hospital):**
1. Inform the insurer within 24 hours of hospitalization
2. Show your policy number and ID at the hospital's insurance desk
3. The hospital sends a pre-authorization request to the insurer
4. The insurer approves — you don't pay (except non-medical expenses)

**Reimbursement (after discharge):**
1. Collect all documents: discharge summary, bills, prescriptions, lab reports
2. Fill out the claim form (download from insurer's website)
3. Submit within 7-15 days of discharge
4. The insurer processes within 7-30 days

### Auto Insurance Claim

1. Ensure safety first — move to a safe location
2. Document the scene — take photos of all vehicles and damage
3. File an FIR if there's injury or significant damage
4. Notify the insurer within 48 hours
5. Submit: FIR copy, claim form, RC book, driving license, photos, repair estimate

## Article 5: Insurance Terms Explained: A Simple Glossary

Insurance documents are full of jargon. Here are the terms you need to know:

| Term | What It Means (in plain English) |
|------|----------------------------------|
| **Sum Insured** | The maximum amount the insurer will pay in a year |
| **Premium** | What you pay for the insurance (annually, monthly, etc.) |
| **Deductible** | What you pay before insurance kicks in |
| **Copay** | A fixed percentage you pay for each claim (e.g., 10% copay on a ₹50,000 bill = you pay ₹5,000) |
| **Waiting Period** | Time you must wait before certain coverages activate |
| **Pre-existing Condition** | A health problem you had before buying the policy |
| **Cashless Claim** | The insurer pays the hospital directly — you don't pay upfront |
| **Reimbursement Claim** | You pay first, then submit bills to the insurer for reimbursement |
| **Network Hospital** | A hospital that has a cashless arrangement with your insurer |
| **No-Claim Bonus** | A discount on your premium for not filing any claims in a year |
| **Rider** | An add-on to your base policy (e.g., critical illness, personal accident) |
| **Exclusion** | Something the policy does NOT cover |
| **Endorsement** | A change or addition to your existing policy |
| **Grace Period** | Extra time to pay your premium without losing coverage |
| **Policy Lapse** | When your policy expires because you didn't pay the premium |
