## THE SURPRISING METAPHOR: PHOTO ALBUM
It's not just cute. It's **structurally correct** because:
1. **It's passive until activated.** Policies sit unused 99% of the time. The product sits too. But when someone dies, when a claim is rejected, when a hospital demands payment — *that's when the album opens.*
2. **It's about recognition, not action.** A photo album doesn't do anything. It enables *seeing*. The product enables *recognizing* the family's protection state. It surfaces: *this is who we are when things go wrong.*
3. **It's about narrative coherence.** Policies aren't isolated documents. They're a story: the father bought this term plan when the daughter was born. The mother has this corporate health cover. The grandfather added this rider. The album makes the story visible.
4. **It survives expansion.** Warranties? In the album. Benefits? In the album. B2B distribution? The company gets its own album.
5. **It sidesteps the "protection" cliché.** Everyone says "protect your family." The album says: *remember who you are*.

**The problem:** "Photo Album" is generic. "Album" is generic. "Memory Box" is archival. "Keepsake" feels dead.
**The opportunity:** Find the *structural equivalent* of the photo album — the thing that makes family self-recognition possible in crisis — without naming the object itself.

## FIVE NAMES FROM THE METAPHORS

### 1. BRIGHTWORK
**Pronunciation:** /ˈbraɪtwɜːrk/ (one word, crisp)
**Type:** Arbitrary metaphor (nautical/industrial)
**Descriptor:** "Brightwork — Your family's protection, made visible."
**Primary tagline:** "See what protects your family."
**The metaphor:** On ships, brightwork is the polished brass, stainless steel, chrome — the metal that gleams. It's not functional. It's not structural. It's the part that *reflects light*, that catches your eye, that tells you the ship is cared for. Dark ship? Neglected. Brightwork shining? Someone is maintaining this vessel.
**Why it wins:**
- **Structurally correct:** The product makes hidden protections *visible* — it brightens what was dark.
- **Ownable:** "Brightwork" is rare in consumer tech. Nautical associations are scattered, not concentrated.
- **Emotionally right:** "Bright" = clarity, optimism. "Work" = substance, craft, care. Together: "this is cared for."
- **Distinctive:** Sounds nothing like "CoverWise," "CoverSure," "Coverfox."
- **Speak + spell:** Hear "brightwork," say "brightwork," type "brightwork." No ambiguity.
- **Expansion-proof:** Warranties, benefits, B2B — all are "work" that can be "brightened" (made visible/coherent).
**What could break it:**
- **Perceived complexity:** Some users might hear "brightwork" and think "photo editing software" or "lighting design."
- **Collision:** There's a marine maintenance company called Brightwork in the US. May not matter in India, but worth checking.
- **Emotional distance:** It's nautical/industrial. Does a grieving widow in Pune feel warmth from "brightwork"?
**Language/collision risks:**
- US: Brightwork exists (marine services). Brightworks exist (lighting/photography).
- India: No obvious conflicts detected in quick mental check — but this is NOT legal clearance.
- App store: "Brightwork" returns scattered results across categories.

## THE THING MOST PEOPLE MISS ABOUT THIS:

**Insurance policies are not financial instruments. They're identity documents.**
A life insurance policy isn't an investment. It's a statement: *when I die, my family will be this okay.* A health policy says: *when we get sick, we can go here.* A warranty says: *when this breaks, we can fix it.*
These are **answers to the question: who are we when things go wrong?**
The product doesn't "manage policies." It **makes the family recognizable to itself in crisis**. When a hospitalization happens, the family doesn't know who they are. Are we covered? Which hospital? What's the limit? The product *answers*: this is who you are. This is what you have. This is what you can do.
Most branding misses this. They focus on "protection," "security," "safety" — abstract nouns that describe *features*, not *identity*. The photo album metaphor works because it's literally about self-recognition. The name "Brightwork" works because it makes identity visible. "Kept" works because it preserves identity. "Ground Truth" works because it resolves identity.
**The insight:** Rename this not as an "insurance app" but as a **family self-recognition system in crisis**. The name that captures that wins.
