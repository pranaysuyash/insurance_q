## 0. Why this document exists

The naming process had two phases that look like one continuous exercise but are actually very different:

1. **Phase 1 — the wide-open panel** (10 roles, ~33 candidate names, rich outputs). It was thorough, well-run, and **partially wrong**. It optimized for a brief the founder had stated but that didn't fully capture what the product is.
2. **Phase 2 — the three founder redirects** (in conversation, after the panel). The founder rejected the panel's top directions one by one, each rejection sharpening the brief. The corrected brief is narrower, cleaner, and points at a different (much smaller) candidate set.

A future reader who looks only at the panel synthesis will reach the wrong conclusion about where the project landed. This document exists so the redirects — which are the actual decision logic — are not lost to chat history. **The most valuable output of this process is the corrected brief, not any single name.**

## 1. The starting brief (what the panel was asked to do)

The panel was dispatched against the founder's voting-pack brief:

- **Product framing:** "an India-first mobile app that reads insurance policies a family already owns, extracts source-cited summaries, answers grounded questions, tracks renewals, finds coverage gaps, keeps emergency/family access ready"
- **Audience:** Indian households (English + Hindi/Tamil/Gujarati/Marathi)
- **Doctrine:** "Name the outcome, object or feeling. Do not name the industry." Insurance words banned from the masterbrand (allowed only in descriptors).
- **Vision:** "a SET of products across same/different markets in the same industry, so the name must be portfolio-reusable"
- **Emotional flavor:** warm, competent, calm
- **Constraint:** avoid the entire "Cover-*" family (CoverWise/CoverSure collision is critical)

Ten roles (Strategist, Champion, Operator, Cartographer, Archivist ×2, Trickster, Skeptic, Future Self, Outsider, Devil's Advocate, Executioner) ran against this brief. Outputs are in `brainstorm_2026-07-28/raw/`.

The panel surfaced real insight — especially the **crisis-state retrievability** test (4 roles converged), the **"kin" root** convergence (4 roles), the **Indic-rooted vs English** tension, and the **"gift not stash"** recovery-flow lens. The full synthesis is in `brainstorm_2026-07-28/README.md`.

**The panel's Tier-1 shortlist:** Northkin, Tippani, Kosha, Amanat, Kept Whole.

## 2. The redirects — what the founder rejected and why

This is the section that matters most. Each redirect killed a direction and sharpened the brief.

### Redirect 1 — "Tippani sounds like a commentary app, not something that helps understand insurance"

The founder's exact words: *"tippani sounds as if its a commentary app not something that would help a user understand their insurances, so think like this what alternate but simpler names exist that make the user understand — so maybe samajh is something people know so what mix can we use for insurance etc?"*

**What this killed:**
- **Tippani** (the Cartographer's #1 and my Tier-1 pick) — *टिप्पणी* literally means "comment/remark/criticism." The founder is right: a "commentary" framing positions the product as an opinion-layer on top of insurance, not as the thing that makes the document comprehensible. The Cartographer was seduced by the "annotation layer" structural metaphor and missed the word's actual connotation.
- **All margin-note / annotation / gloss metaphors** — anything that implies the product *comments on* the document rather than *reads it for you*.

**What the founder redirected toward:** comprehension-vocabulary the audience already knows — *samajh* (understanding), mixed with the category word. The first response proposed **PolicySamajh / BimaSamajh / SamajhDaar** and similar.

### Redirect 2 — "I don't want anything that sounds like it needs legality backing or regulatory"

The founder's exact words: *"but i dont want to get into something that sounds it needs legality backing or regulatory etc"*

**What this killed:**
- **The entire comprehension + category family** — PolicySamajh, BimaSamajh, SahiBima, Insurance Khata, BimaBol, Policy Reader, SamajhDaar-as-evaluator. **All of them.** The reason is structural: pairing "understand/know/right/correct" with "insurance/policy/bima" reads as advisory — "this app will tell you whether your insurance is *right*." Advisory positioning in a regulated category is exactly the boundary the product refuses to cross. The founder's recoil is correct.
- **Sahi** (right/correct) as a root — implies evaluation.
- **Anything that names judgment** — "good measure," "second look," any name suggesting the product assesses adequacy.

This redirect invalidated the entire "name-the-comprehension" direction that 4 panel roles (Operator, Cartographer, Outsider, Archivist) had independently championed. They were optimizing for *warm Hindi household trust* and forgot the regulatory fence.

### Redirect 3 — "Why only Hindi — it's a generic app, functionality is RAG, just that it targets insurance"

The founder's exact words: *"and why only hindi , its not like we would stay local to india only, its a generic app , functionality is rag , just that it targets the insurance"*

**What this killed:**
- **All Indic-rooted names** — Amanat, Bahi, Kosha, Prama, Apta, Lekh, Sambhal, Samjho, Hisaab, Sahaj, Sahi, BimaBol, BimaSamajh, PolicySamajh. The entire School B (vernacular-functional) direction.
- **The India-lock assumption.** The panel (and I) treated "India-first launch" as "India-only product." The founder corrected: the app is a **generic RAG-over-documents tool** that *launches* on insurance, not an India-specific insurance product. A Hindi/Urdu/Sanskrit name is a foreign object in Kenya, Indonesia, Brazil, or a US ex-pat market. The portfolio vision the founder originally stated ("different markets") was the clue the panel under-weighted.

This redirect collapsed Schools A and B — both were wrong because both were anchored to India and to insurance. The real product is neither.

## 3. The corrected brief (the actual output of this process)

After the three redirects, the brief is meaningfully different from what the panel was given. This is the brief any future naming work — or counsel search, or asset creation — must run against.

### What the product actually is (corrected)
A **category-agnostic RAG product** — upload an opaque document, ask questions, get source-cited answers. It *launches* on insurance documents (the first vertical), but the architecture and brand must work on warranties, contracts, medical records, research papers, lease agreements, terms of service, any document domain. It is **not** an insurance product; it is a document-understanding tool that happens to target insurance first.

### Hard constraints for the name (corrected)
1. **Category-agnostic** — must not name or imply insurance, finance, protection, or any single document domain. Must travel into other verticals without renaming.
2. **Globally portable** — must work in any market (India, SE Asia, Africa, English-speaking West). No language lock-in. English is the neutral lingua franca for a multi-market product.
3. **Zero advisory / regulatory signal** — must not imply evaluation, judgment, recommendation, correctness, or advice. The product reads and cites; it does not assess. This rules out *sahi, samajh, right, correct, good, measure, advise, guide, recommend*.
4. **Names the function, not the industry** — the function is RAG: read the document, answer questions, cite the source. The name should name one of those verbs (read / ask / answer / cite / source), not the vertical it's applied to.
5. **Survives crisis-state retrievability** — the binding operational test from the panel (a stressed non-user hears it once, types it correctly). Still applies.
6. **Ownable** — coined or distinctive enough to register in Classes 9/36/42 across jurisdictions.
7. **Non-decorative** — no whimsy for whimsy's sake (the Trickster's photo-album direction is out — it doesn't name the function).

### What this brief rules out (consolidated kill list)
- The entire "Cover-*" family (CoverWise collision — the original reason for renaming).
- All coined soft-vowel marks (Norial/Keralune/Toveli/Avylo pattern — unanimous panel kill).
- All phrase-names kept as phrases (operational fragility).
- All Indic-rooted names (India lock-in).
- All comprehension + category compounds (regulatory/advisory signal).
- All container metaphors (drawer/vault/folder — names the problem, not the solution).
- All navigation metaphors (compass/atlas/beacon — over-commercialized).
- All judgment/evaluation vocabulary (sahi/right/correct/measure — advisory risk).
- "RAG" as a name element — developer jargon; target users have never heard it; and in Indian English "ragging" means college hazing/bullying (strongly negative load). Added via Redirect 4.

## 3.1 The layering reframe (from Redirect 4) — app layer now, portfolio layer later

Redirect 4 split the corrected brief into two layers, decided at two different times. This re-separates the company/portfolio/app layers that `rename_strategy_and_inventory_2026-07-28.md` §1 originally documented but that the panel and Redirects 1–3 had re-collapsed.

- **App layer (decide now):** insurance-specific, because that is what the app *does today*. The app name carries the domain (insurance/policy) + the function (ask/answer/question), with no advisory signal.
- **Portfolio/umbrella layer (decide later):** generic RAG brand (e.g. AskSource), decided when product #2 is real. This is a one-line decision at that point, not a multi-round exercise.

This is what the Executioner's kill-test condition #4 demanded ("Drop the portfolio constraint for v1. Name the app"). The founder re-asserted it after the agent had drifted back toward forcing one name to serve both layers.

**Implication for the candidate set:** the app-layer candidates are now insurance-specific functional compounds (InsAsk, PolicyQ, etc.), not the generic RAG names (Sourced/Pageback/Cited) that Redirects 1–3 had produced. The generic names are *not killed* — they are deferred to the portfolio layer.

## 5. What was learned (the process insights, not the names)

These are the durable takeaways that should inform any future naming or product work, regardless of which name is chosen:

### 5.1 The panel optimized for a brief that was partially wrong
The founder's voting-pack brief said "India-first insurance app, name the outcome, portfolio-reusable." The panel ran hard against that. But the founder's later redirects revealed the brief had under-specified two things: (a) the product is a *generic RAG tool*, not an insurance product, and (b) it must *travel globally*, not just pan-India. Four roles (Operator, Cartographer, Outsider, Archivist) invested heavily in Indic-rooted names that the founder then killed with one sentence ("why only Hindi"). The lesson: **the brief's framing determines the output more than the panel's quality. A great panel against a wrong brief produces great wrong answers.**

### 5.2 Convergence is not correctness
The panel's strongest convergence was "the kin root" (4 roles) and "crisis-state retrievability" (4 roles). Both are real insights. But the kin-root convergence pointed at names (Northkin, Kinset, Kinsight, Kinloom) that are English-portfolio-aspirational — which is a *founder-pleasing* direction, not necessarily a user-correct one. And the retrievability insight, while true, applies to *any* name and doesn't narrow the field. **Convergence measures agreement, not truth.** The founder's redirects were a higher-quality signal than the panel's convergence.

### 5.3 The founder's redirects were the actual decision logic
The panel generated options. The founder's three rejections — "commentary, not comprehension," "no regulatory signal," "generic RAG, not India-insurance" — did more to sharpen the answer than all 10 roles combined. This is the pattern: **generative divergence (panel) + decisive convergence (founder) is the right division of labor.** The panel's job was to surface the space; the founder's job was to cut it. Both did their job; the cutting is what produced the answer.

### 5.4 The product definition was clarified by the naming process
The most valuable output of this exercise may not be a name at all — it's the sharpened product definition: **"a category-agnostic RAG product that launches on insurance."** That sentence was latent in the founder's head; the naming process forced it to the surface. Future product, positioning, and architecture decisions should run against this corrected definition, not the original "India-first insurance app" framing.

### 5.5 The Doctrine was wrong for this product
The voting pack's doctrine — "Name the outcome, object or feeling. Do not name the industry." — is a sound general principle (it produced Lemonade, Digit, Acko). But it was misapplied here: the founder heard "name the outcome" as "name a warm emotional state" (North & Kin, Held Together, Steady), when for a *functional RAG tool* the right application is "name the function the user performs" (read, ask, cite, source). The Devil's Advocate tried to make this point ("name the function") and was overruled by the doctrine's framing. The redirects vindicated the Devil's Advocate. **Lesson: doctrines need to be re-examined against the specific product, not applied as universal rules.**

### 5.6 Layer collapse is a recurring failure mode (from Redirect 4)
The rename strategy doc (§1) documented three naming layers — company / portfolio / app. The panel collapsed them into one (seeking a single "portfolio-reusable" name). Redirects 1–3 stayed collapsed (seeking one generic name). Redirect 4 re-separated them: *app-specific now, portfolio-generic later.* This is the second time in the project that layer-collapse caused a wrong direction (the first was the original CoverWise-doing-double-duty-as-both-app-and-portfolio). **Lesson: when a naming or architecture decision feels strained, check whether layers have been collapsed that should be separate.** Deciding the app name now and the portfolio name later is cheaper and more honest than forcing one name to serve both.

## 6. Anything else? (motto §0.1.1)

1. **The brainstorm docs are not invalidated by the redirects.** The panel's raw outputs and synthesis remain valuable — the operational tests (retrievability, voice-to-text), the collision findings (do-not-repitch ledger), the kill-test logic (Executioner), and the structural insights (Cartographer's "reference layer not container") all still apply. Only the *Indic-rooted and outcome-emotional* candidate directions are killed. Future naming work should build on the panel's *methods*, not its *candidates*.

2. **The candidate set is deliberately small and now layered.** Post-Redirect-4, the app-layer set is 4 candidates (InsAsk, PolicyQ, InsQ, PolicyAsk) and the portfolio-layer set is parked (Sourced, Pageback, Cited, AskSource). The founder should not request "more app-layer options" without first rejecting these four on their merits; if these are wrong, the brief is still wrong, and more candidates won't fix that. The layering (specific-app-now, generic-portfolio-later) is itself a decision that should not be re-litigated without new information (e.g. product #2 becoming real).

3. **The voice-to-text test still applies.** Even for English functional names, the test (5 speakers, Indian-English + accents, Google voice typing on a mid-range Android) is the cheapest, highest-leverage validation. "Sourced" and "Cited" are common enough that voice-to-text will return them; "Pageback" is coined and needs the test specifically.

4. **No name is legally cleared.** Stated for the fourth time. Counsel + IP India + USPTO + EUIPO searches (Classes 9, 36, 42) are the binding gate before any commitment. The corrected candidates are common English words/compounds, so trademark defense will lean on distinctive wordmark + secondary meaning — the same model LIC, CRED, Stripe, Slack used.

5. **The "Sourced" pick has a specific weakness worth flagging.** "Sourced" is a past participle; it reads as a state ("the answers are sourced") rather than a noun or verb. Some founders find past-participle brands (Slack, Stripe are nouns; "Sourced" is not) slightly less satisfying as a *thing* you can point at. If the founder reacts to "Sourced" with "it's a description, not a name," that's the same instinct, and Pageback (a coined noun) is the fix. Both are defensible; the difference is aesthetic.

6. **The decision should be made now, not refined further.** The Executioner's kill-test verdict applies: the rename survived on "permanent later, cheap now," but the elaborate process inverts that economics. Three more rounds of candidate generation would be the trap the Executioner warned about. The corrected brief is sharp; the candidate set is small; the next step is founder judgment + counsel search, not more brainstorming.

## 7. Decision gate (what locks in the name)

Per the clearance report's release gate (adapted to the corrected brief), the name is locked when **all** are true:

1. The founder picks **one** of the corrected candidates (or rejects all three and sharpens the brief further — but does not expand the candidate set without first rejecting these).
2. Counsel completes **official trademark search** (IP India Classes 9/36/42 + USPTO + EUIPO for global portability; exact + phonetic).
3. The survivor passes the **voice-to-text test** (5 speakers, multi-accent).
4. **Domains + core handles** are secured before any public mention.
5. **No public release** (store, press, partnership, repo rename) until 1–4 are done. "CoverWise" remains the internal codename until then.

The mechanical rename (per `rename_strategy_and_inventory_2026-07-28.md`) is one gated afternoon once the name is locked. Counsel search is the only step worth real calendar time.
