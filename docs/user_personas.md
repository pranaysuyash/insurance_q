# User Personas - Insurance Policy Parser & QA App

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Last Updated:** June 19, 2025  
**Created By:** Product Team Analysis  

## Executive Summary

This document defines the primary user personas for the Insurance Policy Parser & QA Application based on analysis of the current codebase, features, and user flows. These personas represent the target users who will benefit most from the app's AI-powered document analysis and natural language question-answering capabilities.

The application serves users who need simplified access to complex insurance information, with a particular focus on mobile accessibility and family insurance management.

---

## Primary Persona: The Overwhelmed Policyholder

### Persona: Sarah Chen
**Age:** 34  
**Occupation:** Marketing Manager  
**Location:** Suburban area  
**Tech Comfort:** Moderate  

#### Background & Context
Sarah has multiple insurance policies (health, auto, renters, life) but finds insurance documents confusing and overwhelming. She dreads having to dig through policy documents to find specific information, especially when she needs answers quickly. She primarily uses her smartphone for personal tasks and prefers simple, intuitive apps.

#### Demographics
- **Income:** $65,000 annually
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree
- **Family Status:** Married, 2 young children
- **Device Usage:** Primarily smartphone (iPhone), occasional tablet use
- **Insurance Complexity:** 4-6 active policies across different insurers

#### Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Quickly understand what her insurance covers without reading entire policy documents
- **Secondary Goals:**
  - Find specific coverage details when needed (deductibles, copays, coverage limits)
  - Understand insurance terminology without feeling confused
  - Manage family insurance information in one place
  - Get answers to insurance questions in plain English

#### Pain Points & Frustrations
- **Information Overload:** Insurance documents are too long and written in confusing legal language
- **Time Constraints:** Doesn't have time to read through entire policies to find specific information
- **Multiple Documents:** Keeps losing track of which policy covers what
- **Technical Jargon:** Gets confused by insurance terminology (deductibles, copays, coinsurance, etc.)
- **Accessibility:** Often needs information while on-the-go but policies are stored in email or filing cabinets

#### Technology Comfort & Preferences
- **Comfort Level:** Moderate - comfortable with standard apps but prefers simple interfaces
- **Device Preference:** Mobile-first user - expects apps to work seamlessly on her phone
- **Learning Style:** Visual learner who prefers quick summaries over detailed explanations
- **App Usage:** Uses apps for banking, shopping, social media, and basic productivity

#### How Sarah Uses the Insurance App
1. **Document Upload:** Takes photos of insurance documents or uploads PDFs from email
2. **Quick Questions:** Uses the pre-set questions feature to get immediate answers about coverage
3. **Terminology Help:** Frequently references the insurance glossary when encountering unfamiliar terms
4. **Family Management:** Tracks insurance for herself, spouse, and children in the family section
5. **Mobile Access:** Primarily uses the app on her phone during commutes or while waiting

#### Key User Scenarios
- **Scenario 1:** At the doctor's office, quickly checks her health insurance copay for specialist visits
- **Scenario 2:** After a minor car accident, needs to find her auto insurance deductible and claims process
- **Scenario 3:** Shopping for additional life insurance, wants to understand her current coverage limits
- **Scenario 4:** Annual enrollment period - comparing her current health plan benefits with new options

#### Success Metrics for Sarah
- Time to find specific policy information: < 2 minutes
- Comprehension of insurance terms: 80% understanding without external help
- App usage frequency: 2-3 times per month
- User satisfaction: High confidence in insurance knowledge

## User Insights & Implications

### Key Behavioral Patterns
1. **Mobile-First Usage:** All personas prefer mobile access for quick information retrieval
2. **Simplicity Over Features:** Users value ease-of-use over comprehensive functionality
3. **Context-Driven Queries:** Most questions arise from specific life situations (doctor visits, accidents, etc.)
4. **Family-Centric Needs:** Insurance decisions often involve multiple family members
5. **Time-Sensitive Information:** Users need quick access to specific details when situations arise

### Application Feature Alignment
- **Natural Language Q&A:** Directly addresses users' need to understand complex policy language
- **Mobile Flutter App:** Matches user preference for mobile-first insurance access
- **Family Management:** Supports users managing multiple family member policies
- **Document Categorization:** Helps users organize multiple policy types
- **Insurance Glossary:** Addresses universal confusion about insurance terminology
- **Pre-set Questions:** Provides quick access to common insurance queries

### Design Implications
1. **Prioritize Mobile Experience:** Ensure all core features work seamlessly on mobile devices
2. **Simplify Language:** Use plain English in all user-facing content
3. **Visual Hierarchy:** Use clear visual design to highlight important information
4. **Quick Actions:** Provide shortcuts for common tasks (upload, quick questions)
5. **Progressive Disclosure:** Show basic information first, with details available on demand

### Feature Recommendations
1. **Notification System:** Reminder alerts for renewal dates and important policy changes
2. **Emergency Mode:** Quick access to essential policy information during emergencies
3. **Sharing Capabilities:** Allow users to share policy summaries with family members
4. **Offline Access:** Cache important policy information for offline viewing
5. **Claims Assistant:** Guided workflows for common claim types
