# Insurance Terminology Glossary

This glossary provides definitions for common insurance terms that users may encounter in their insurance policies and within the application.

## A

**Actual Cash Value (ACV)**: The cost to replace damaged or destroyed property with new property of similar quality minus depreciation.

**Actuary**: A professional who analyzes the financial costs of risk and uncertainty using mathematics, statistics, and financial theory.

**Adjuster**: A person who investigates and settles insurance claims.

**Adverse Selection**: The tendency of higher-risk individuals to seek insurance coverage more actively than lower-risk individuals.

**Agent**: A licensed insurance company representative who sells policies on behalf of the insurer.

## B

**Beneficiary**: The person or entity designated to receive the proceeds or benefits from a life insurance policy when the insured dies.

**Benefit Period**: The length of time an insured person is eligible to receive benefits for a covered illness or injury.

**Binding**: A temporary agreement that provides insurance coverage until the actual policy is issued or declined.

**Broker**: A licensed insurance professional who represents the insured rather than the insurance company.

## C

**Claim**: A formal request to an insurance company for payment based on the terms of the policy.

**COBRA (Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act)**: A federal law that allows employees to continue their health insurance coverage after leaving employment.

**Coinsurance**: The percentage of costs of a covered health care service you pay after you've paid your deductible.

**Copay/Copayment**: A fixed amount you pay for a covered health care service, usually when you receive the service.

**Coverage Limit**: The maximum amount an insurance company will pay for a covered loss.

## D

**Deductible**: The amount you pay for covered health care services before your insurance plan starts to pay.

**Depreciation**: The decrease in the value of property over time due to wear and tear or obsolescence.

**Disability Insurance**: Insurance that provides income if you are unable to work due to illness or injury.

## E

**Endorsement**: A written amendment attached to an insurance policy that modifies the policy's coverage, terms, or conditions.

**Exclusion**: A provision in an insurance policy that eliminates coverage for certain risks, perils, properties, or locations.

**EOB (Explanation of Benefits)**: A statement from your insurance company showing what medical treatments and services were paid for.

## F

**Face Value**: The amount of the death benefit in a life insurance policy.

**Family Deductible**: In health insurance, the total amount that a family must pay out-of-pocket before the health plan begins to pay.

**First-Party Coverage**: Insurance that covers the policyholder's own losses or damages.

## G

**Grace Period**: A specified period after a premium payment is due in which the policyholder may make the payment without penalty or lapse of coverage.

**Group Insurance**: A single insurance policy covering multiple individuals, typically issued to an employer or organization.

## H

**Health Savings Account (HSA)**: A tax-advantaged savings account designed to help people with high-deductible health plans pay for out-of-pocket medical expenses.

**High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)**: A health insurance plan with a higher deductible than a traditional insurance plan and typically lower premiums.

## I

**Indemnity**: The principle that an insured should not profit from a loss but be restored to approximately the same financial position as before the loss.

**In-Network Provider**: A healthcare provider who has contracted with an insurance company to provide services to plan members at pre-negotiated rates.

**Insurance Score**: A rating used by insurance companies that is based on a consumer's credit history.

## L

**Lapse**: The termination of an insurance policy because of failure to pay the premium.

**Liability Insurance**: Insurance that protects against claims alleging that one's negligence or inappropriate action resulted in bodily injury or property damage.

**Long-Term Care Insurance**: Insurance that helps pay for extended care services in a variety of settings such as nursing homes or in-home care.

## M

**Medicaid**: A state and federal program that provides health coverage for some low-income people, families and children, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities.

**Medicare**: A federal health insurance program for people who are 65 or older, certain younger people with disabilities, and people with End-Stage Renal Disease.

## N

**Network**: The facilities, providers, and suppliers your health insurer has contracted with to provide health care services.

## O

**Open Enrollment Period**: A time period during which eligible individuals can enroll in health insurance or make changes to their existing coverage.

**Out-of-Network Provider**: A healthcare provider who has not contracted with an insurance company for reimbursement at a negotiated rate.

**Out-of-Pocket Maximum**: The most you have to pay for covered services in a plan year. After you spend this amount on deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance, your health plan pays 100% of the costs of covered benefits.

## P

**Pre-Existing Condition**: A health problem you had before the date that new health coverage starts.

**Premium**: The amount paid for an insurance policy, typically in monthly installments.

**Prior Authorization**: A decision by your health insurer that a health care service, treatment plan, prescription drug, or durable medical equipment is medically necessary.

## Q

**Qualified Medical Expense**: Expenses that generally would qualify for the medical and dental expenses deduction on your tax return.

## R

**Rider**: An addition to an insurance policy that changes the policy's coverage, terms, or conditions.

**Risk**: The chance of loss or the person or entity that is insured.

**Replacement Cost**: The cost to replace damaged property with new property of similar quality without deducting for depreciation.

## S

**Self-Insurance**: The practice of setting aside money to cover potential losses instead of purchasing insurance.

**Subrogation**: The right of an insurer to pursue a third party that caused an insurance loss to the insured.

**Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)**: A document that insurance companies must provide that describes what a plan covers and what it costs.

## T

**Term Life Insurance**: Life insurance that provides coverage for a specified time period and pays a benefit only if the insured dies during that period.

**Third-Party Administrator (TPA)**: An organization that processes insurance claims and performs other administrative services.

## U

**Underwriting**: The process by which an insurance company examines, accepts, or rejects insurance risks, and classifies those selected, in order to charge the proper premium.

**Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Coverage**: Insurance that protects you if you're in an accident with a driver who doesn't have insurance or doesn't have enough insurance.

## W

**Waiting Period**: The time that must pass before coverage becomes effective or benefits are payable.

**Whole Life Insurance**: A type of permanent life insurance that provides coverage for the entire lifetime of the insured and includes a savings component.
